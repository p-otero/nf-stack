# 3D: model viewer and model scan

`ModelViewer` (the `model-viewer` item) shows a glTF or GLB people can turn. `ModelScan` (`model-scan`) shows a photo turning into a 3D character while your engine works. Neither runs a model or fetches anything but the model URL you pass.

## Cost

The viewer's shell (stage, poster, controls, the primitive) is about 74 KB gzip, two thirds of it UI SDK shared with every item. three.js and react-three-fiber are a separate chunk of a few hundred KB, imported only when a stage nears the viewport (200 px before it), only with WebGL, and never on a page without a viewer. `preloadModelViewer()` starts it early, like on hover over a link to the page. A `ModelScan` starts it at `detecting`, so it's there by `building`; its transition (the cloud) is a second small chunk loaded then. With its controls, `ModelScan`'s shell is about 87 KB gzip.

## ModelViewer

```tsx
<ModelViewer
  model="/models/figure.glb" // a URL, its bytes (ArrayBuffer), or a loaded { scene, animations }
  alt="A stylised figure in a teal shirt" // required: the stage is an image to assistive tech
  poster="/models/figure.png" // until it loads, and without WebGL
  summary="A standing figure with short hair. Clips: Idle, Wave." // a visible text alternative
  aspectRatio={3 / 4}
  weights={{ Smile: 0.6 }} // morph targets by name, 0 to 1
  onModelLoad={(scene) => restyle(scene)} // before the first frame; may return a cleanup, or a promise of one
/>
```

- **Camera.** Drag turns, wheel and pinch zoom; with the stage focused, arrows turn, + and - zoom, Home resets. `view` / `defaultView` / `onViewChange` take `front`, `three-quarter`, `side`, `back`, or `null` after a free turn. `framing="full"` fits the model; `framing={{ center: [x, y, z], radius }}` (the model's own units) frames a part, like the face.
- **Clips.** `clip` / `defaultClip` / `onClipChange` (the first clip by default), `playing` / `defaultPlaying` / `onPlayingChange` (left out or `null`: plays, except under reduced motion), `loop`, `speed`. A play toggle and a native select show when the model has clips.
- **Weights** are a `Float32Array` (or numbers) aligned with `targetNames`, or a map by name. `targetNames` default to the GLB's own `extras.targetNames` (meshes in scene order). Values aren't clamped: a principal component can be negative. `expressions={{ mouthSmileLeft: 0.8 }}` sets ARKit expressions on the `expr.<name>` targets, over `weights`. They ease in the render loop, written straight into `morphTargetInfluences` with no allocation: a new `weights` renders React once, never per frame, so stream them as often as your engine produces them. A target you never set is left to clips.
- **Scaled targets.** A GLB can store each morph target scaled to fill its quantized range and name the multipliers in a mesh's `extras.targetScales`, one per target, beside `extras.targetNames`. ModelViewer multiplies every weight and expression by its target's, so you send them in the targets' own units (an ARKit value from 0 to 1). When your engine sends `weights` already multiplied, pass `scaledWeights`; expressions are multiplied either way.
- **Styling a model.** `onModelLoad(scene, animations)` runs once per loaded model, before its first frame: restyle its materials (GLTFLoader puts each material's glTF `extras` in `material.userData`), add outline meshes, hide parts. Return a function to free what it made; it runs when the model goes. It may return a promise, and the model shows once it resolves, so code that imports three.js loads with the 3D chunk: `onModelLoad={async (scene) => (await import("./style")).styleCharacter(scene)}`. ModelViewer reads no engine's extras itself. An engine that restyles a character (another look, other hair) hands you another GLB: pass it as `model` and it replaces the shown one, keeping the camera, the weights and the clip time.
- **A new model** (a refined character) loads under the one on screen and takes over its camera, weights and clip time. It replaces it at once when the two look the same (same meshes, vertex counts, targets and colours), or fades in over it otherwise; at once under reduced motion.
- **Look.** `material="toon"` for a three-tone shade on any GLB (a model with styles of its own takes `onModelLoad` instead); `background="transparent"` to show what's behind; the stage takes `bg-muted` otherwise, so it follows the theme, dark mode and glass. `autoRotate` (true or degrees a second) turns it while nobody drags; never under reduced motion.
- **Rendering.** It draws only while the camera, a clip or a weight moves, and not at all off screen or in a hidden tab. Under reduced motion camera moves jump.
- **No WebGL, or a failed load:** the poster stays with a message (`labels.unsupported`, `error`), and the failure is announced at once.
- **Floor.** `floor` draws a grid disc and a contact shadow under the model's feet in the theme's muted foreground, and pools the card colour over the stage. Theme colours in the canvas are read again whenever the theme changes.
- **Your own controls.** `controls="gestures"` keeps drag, wheel, pinch and keys on the stage without the bar under it. `handleRef` hands over the viewer's handle; `useModelViewerControls(handle)` gives its `status`, `view`, `clips`, `clip` and `playing`, and renders only when one of those changes (never while the camera turns). Drive it with `handle.api.setView(view, event)`, `play`, `pause`, `zoom`, `reset`.
- `controls={false}` hides the controls and makes the stage still and unfocusable; `load={false}` holds the scene back; `overlay` puts DOM over the canvas; `sceneChildren` puts your own three.js content inside it, reading `ModelViewerSceneContext` (the viewer's handle, the model's rig and the `floor`) from `model-viewer-rig`.

Agents find the viewer by `alt`, with the clips as items, and call `rotate(yaw, pitch?)`, `zoom(steps)`, `reset`, `setView(view)`, `play`, `pause` and `selectClip(name)`. Each throws with the reason before the model has loaded or for a clip it doesn't have.

## ModelScan

Drive it from your engine's events. `stage` is the UI's: map your engine's stages onto it.

| Prop            | From the engine                                                                                   |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `stage`         | `photo` → `detecting` → `building` → `refining` → `ready`, or `error`                             |
| `points`        | landmark groups `{ source, points, aspect?, edges? }` (a `Float32Array` of x, y pairs), or a list |
| `masks`         | person masks `{ source?, width, height, data }`: 0–255 per pixel, row-major, the photo's aspect   |
| `model`         | `{ glb, targetNames }`, the base model from `building`, then the final character when it's done   |
| `weights`       | a `Float32Array` aligned with `targetNames`, streamed while refining, then the character's        |
| `scaledWeights` | `true` when those weights come multiplied by the GLB's `extras.targetScales` already              |
| `expressions`   | ARKit weights by name, like a smile once it's ready                                               |
| `progress`      | 0 to 1 within the stage; leave it out while unknown                                               |
| `error`         | what a person reads at `error`: your words for the engine's error code                            |

Points are fractions of the original photo: x of its width, y of its height. `edges` are pairs of point indices a line joins, so a group draws as a mesh; a `body` group of the 33 pose landmarks has the pose skeleton by default, and a group with edges draws only the points they join. They're drawn straight onto the photo where the stage shows it, following `fit` (`contain`, or `cover` in a stage of another `aspectRatio`). Points past 0 or 1 (a landmark a crop cut off; detectors still report it) are kept but not drawn, and don't count in a group's stagger. `aspect` is the photo's own width over height: it sizes the stage only until the photo loads, when `width` and `height` aren't given. Each group lights up on its own, in the order its source arrived; a later group with the same `source` replaces it.

`masks` pick the person: the body mask (or one of the photo's aspect, like a face mask over the face photo) dims the background around them while detecting and decides which pixels fly onto the model. Without a mask, a hull around the body's 33 pose landmarks stands in; without those, every pixel flies. Pass the mask when the engine sends it, as a new array.

ModelScan knows no engine's error codes: map them to your own words and pass `error`. Leave `onRetry` out when a new run can't help, like a browser without WebGL2: say so with ModelViewer's own line for that (`modelViewerLabels.unsupported`). Cancelling through an `AbortSignal` isn't an error; ignore the `AbortError`.

```tsx
// What a person reads per error code; pass your own to translate. The engine's `message` is for logs.
const errorMessages: Record<EngineErrorCode, string> = {
  "no-face": "No face found. Try a photo that faces the camera.",
  "unreadable-image": "This photo can't be read. Try a JPEG or PNG.",
  "webgl2-unavailable": modelViewerLabels.unsupported, // ModelViewer's no-WebGL line; a new run can't help
  "asset-integrity": "The 3D engine's files didn't load correctly. Try again.",
  "asset-load": "The 3D engine couldn't load. Check the connection and try again.",
  worker: "The 3D engine stopped. Try again.",
  internal: "Something went wrong making the 3D model. Try again.",
}

// The engine's last events: { stage }, every { landmarks }, { model }, { progress }, { done: { character } }, and
// { error: { code, message } } as the last one of a failed run.
function toModelScanProps(state: EngineState, messages: Partial<typeof errorMessages> = {}) {
  return {
    stage: (
      {
        idle: "photo",
        detect: "detecting",
        fit: "building",
        refine: "refining",
        done: "ready",
        error: "error",
      } as const
    )[state.stage],
    points: state.landmarks, // [{ source: "face", points: Float32Array, aspect }, { source: "body", … }]: of the photo
    model: state.character ?? state.model, // { glb, targetNames }: the character fades in over the base model
    weights: state.character?.weights ?? state.progress?.weights,
    progress: state.stage === "refine" ? state.progress?.value : undefined,
    error: state.error && (messages[state.error.code] ?? errorMessages[state.error.code]),
    expressions: state.stage === "done" ? { mouthSmileLeft: 0.8, mouthSmileRight: 0.8 } : undefined,
  }
}

;<ModelScan
  src={photo.url}
  alt="Your photo"
  width={photo.width}
  height={photo.height}
  {...toModelScanProps(state)}
  modelAlt="Your 3D character"
  masks={state.masks} // the engine's mask events, when it sends them
  onStart={() => start(photo)} // Make 3D over the photo
  onCancel={running ? () => controller.abort() : undefined} // the run rejects with an AbortError: ignore it
  onRetry={state.error?.code === "webgl2-unavailable" ? undefined : () => start(photo)}
  onStartOver={() => reset()} // back to the photo, from the model or an error
  onDownload={() => save(state.character)} // the GLB you already have
  styles={[
    { value: "soft", label: "Soft" },
    { value: "cel", label: "Cel" },
  ]}
  selectedStyle={style}
  onStyleChange={(next) => restyle(next)} // pass the restyled GLB as `model` when the engine sends it
/>
```

- At `building` the photo moves into the 3D scene on a plane the front camera sees exactly where the 2D photo was. Then the person's pixels fly on curved, staggered paths onto the model's surface while the camera turns to the three-quarter view: they turn the info colour mid-flight and the surface's colour as they land, a shimmer runs down the body revealing the model, the background drifts out of focus, and a pulse runs across the floor. One draw call, uniforms per frame, about 3.4 s. Under reduced motion the photo crossfades into the model instead, and the progress shows throughout.
- **Controls** float over the stage (`controls="overlay"`, the default) as glass bars, or sit under it (`controls="below"`). Over the photo: the style picker and Make 3D (`onStart`). While working: a progress ring, the stage's label (`statusText`), "Step n of 4", the percent and Cancel (`onCancel`). Over the finished model: the style picker and one toolbar of views, the clip (`labels.play`, like "Wave"), Download (`onDownload`) and Start over (`onStartOver`), icon buttons with tooltips. At `error`: the message with Try again (`onRetry`) and Start over. Each control shows only with its callback; `actions` adds your own beside them. Every word is in `labels`.
- At `ready` the idle clip plays (unless motion is reduced); the viewer keeps drag and keys without its own bar. Pass more viewer props through `viewer` (`framing`, `material`, `floor={false}`, `summary`, `labels`).
- When the control that had focus goes with the stage (Make 3D as the run begins, Cancel as it ends), focus moves to the stage's main control (Cancel, Try again, Make 3D), never the page.
- Agents call `start`, `cancel`, `retry`, `restart` and `download`. A call the stage doesn't offer, or without its callback, fails the step with why; a change your callback refuses with `details.cancel()` fails it too.
- Each stage is announced (`labels.stage`), progress at most every tenth and two seconds, errors at once.
